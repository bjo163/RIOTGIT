# R!OTGIT — Brand Identity

## Brand lockup

**R!OTGIT**  
**THE UNDERGROUND CODE NETWORK**  
**BREAK THE RULES. OWN YOUR CODE.**

The exclamation mark is intentional: it is the visual spark and interruption in the wordmark. Write the product name as **R!OTGIT** in display contexts and **Riotgit** only where a system requires a conventional title case name. Do not silently normalize it to "Riot Git".

## Brand essence

**Sovereign code. Unfiltered craft. Collective rebellion.**

R!OTGIT is a place for builders who want control over their infrastructure and freedom over their workflow. "Riot" means challenging platform lock-in and unnecessary complexity — never careless engineering, insecure defaults, or disrespect toward other people.

## Positioning

A self-hosted and distributed-ready Git forge for independent builders and teams who want the power of a modern code platform without surrendering ownership of their repositories.

### Brand differentiators
- Ownable: self-hosted, portable, backed by standard Git repositories.
- Unboxed: browser, terminal, API, and network are equal surfaces.
- Unfiltered: unmistakable extreme visual identity.
- Trustable: security, compatibility, and operational honesty over hype.
- Expandable: modular core with optional network and automation capabilities.

## Audience feeling

When using R!OTGIT, people should feel:
- in control rather than trapped;
- energized rather than overwhelmed;
- part of a builder culture rather than a corporate workflow;
- safe to experiment without hiding operational risk;
- respected as a developer, whether they use a terminal or a browser.

## Voice and writing

**Voice:** punchy, sharp, human, irreverent, technically precise.

Use:
- short headlines;
- active verbs;
- clear technical language;
- playful microcopy in low-risk areas;
- direct warnings in security and destructive actions.

Avoid:
- corporate SaaS filler ("empower your journey");
- fake hacker jargon;
- excessive profanity;
- jokes in permission, deletion, billing, security, or recovery flows;
- claims that a feature is decentralized, secure, production-ready, or compatible until tested.

### Examples
- Good: "Your repo. Your rules."
- Good: "Push it. Review it. Ship it."
- Good: "Network sync is paused. Your local repository is safe."
- Bad: "Revolutionizing the future of collaborative synergy."
- Bad: "Unhackable decentralized Git."
- Bad: "Oopsie, nuked it!" for a destructive operation.

## Visual direction: EXTREME

Keywords:
- photocopied punk zine;
- industrial warning labels;
- xerox grain and halftone;
- torn-paper edges and hard cuts;
- oversized condensed display type;
- acid-lime signal over coal-black surfaces;
- hazard-orange interruption;
- technical grids, registration marks, and monospace metadata;
- sharp comic motion lines used sparingly.

The visual system must be bold but not chaotic. Content hierarchy, code readability, and accessibility always win over texture.

## Logo direction

### Primary mark
A custom geometric **R!** symbol:
- heavy angular R silhouette;
- a cut / slash representing disruption and forward motion;
- an exclamation stroke/dot treated as a signal marker;
- stable silhouette at 16–24 px;
- one-color version must work without gradients or texture.

### Wordmark
- uppercase `R!OTGIT`;
- condensed, heavy, forward-leaning display treatment;
- tight tracking but clear letter separation;
- the exclamation mark should remain legible, not look like an accidental lowercase l;
- do not rely on a custom font being installed. Supply text as text where possible and keep SVG paths/shapes self-contained.

### Clear space and misuse
- Leave at least the height of the exclamation dot around the mark.
- Never stretch, rotate, outline, or apply multiple competing glows.
- Do not put the wordmark over noisy imagery without a solid backing.
- Do not recolor status indicators to match brand colors when that harms meaning.
- Do not use the mark as a substitute for accessible text labels.

## Imagery and motion

Use high-contrast editorial compositions, abstract signal distortion, analog print textures, close-up industrial materials, code fragments, and hand-drawn motion marks. Avoid stock "hooded hacker" clichés, fake terminal screenshots, and generic neon city wallpapers.

Motion should be short and purposeful: snap transitions, wipe reveals, small glitch accents. Never animate code continuously or flash rapidly. Respect reduced-motion preferences.
